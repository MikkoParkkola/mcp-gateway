## A firewall rule's `scan` list is honoured

**Startup:** no notice

Before 4.0.0 a rule's `scan` list was ignored. A rule whose `match` covered the tool decided every finding in the call, so `{match: "*", scan: [credentials], action: allow}` also allowed a shell injection in the same call. Now each finding is decided by the first rule whose `match` covers the tool and whose `scan` list holds that finding's type, or else by its severity default. The call or answer takes the most restrictive result. A rule without `scan` decides every type, as before.

- What you lose: an `allow` or `warn` rule with a `scan` list no longer softens findings of other types.
- What you gain: a rule does what its `scan` list says, and an allowance for one finding type cannot carry another through.
