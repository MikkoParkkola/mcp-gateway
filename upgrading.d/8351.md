## The per-backend route checks every tool call at dispatch, as `/mcp` does

**Startup:** no notice

**A `tools/call` on `/mcp/{name}` is re-checked just before it is sent.**
- What you lose: a retry whose answers (`inputResponses`) the request firewall blocks is
  refused (-32600) instead of being sent; a call whose authorization was withdrawn between
  its question and its retry is refused at the retry.
- What stays the same: a clean call and a clean retry are sent once, as before. Other
  methods (`tools/list`, `ping`, `prompts/get`, ...) are forwarded as before.
- What you gain: the answers to a backend's question are scanned before they reach it, on
  this route as on `/mcp`.
- What you do: nothing.

**`arguments` sent as a JSON string reach the backend as an object.**
- What you lose: a backend that read `arguments` as a JSON string now receives the parsed
  object. A string that parses to something other than an object (an array, a number) is
  refused with -32602 and nothing is sent.
- What stays the same: `arguments` sent as an object, or left out, are forwarded unchanged.
- What you gain: the firewall scans string-form arguments as the object they are, so a
  payload it blocks in object form is blocked in string form too.
- What you do: nothing, unless a backend of yours expects a JSON string; send it an object.

**A `tools/call` spelled in another letter case is refused.**
- What you lose: `TOOLS/CALL`, `Tools/Call` and other case variants on `/mcp/{name}` are
  answered -32601 (method not found) instead of being forwarded to the backend.
- What stays the same: `tools/call` in lowercase, as every MCP client sends it.
- What you gain: no spelling of a tool call can reach a backend without the dispatch check.
- What you do: send `tools/call` in lowercase.
