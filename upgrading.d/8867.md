## HTTP stop: `server.shutdown_timeout` is now one total, with a 9 s minimum

**Startup:** no notice; refuses to start, only over HTTP (plain or mTLS) with
`server.shutdown_timeout` below 9 s, with this message, before it binds the listener:

> server.shutdown_timeout is 5s, below the 9s an HTTP stop needs for the listener grace,
> the saves, the backend stop and the logger flush; set it to at least 9s

(the first value is the one you set). A stdio gateway is unchanged: it keeps its own bound
and its own small values. The check runs when the HTTP gateway starts serving, so a
managed-account store may already have been opened by then.

What you gain: the gateway's stop stages are bounded by one number. Before, the listener,
the state saves, the in-flight drain, the task drain and the backend stop each started their
own timeout of `server.shutdown_timeout`, one after another, so a stop could take several
times what the setting said. Now the setting is the total from the stop signal, and every
stage is given its share of what is left.

What this does not yet cover: after the gateway's stages end, the process still waits up to
10 s for blocking-pool work before it exits, outside this total. A later change folds that
wait into the same budget; until then, do not size an external kill deadline (a container's
stop grace, a service manager's timeout) from this setting alone.

What changes: the total is split into fixed shares: 250 ms is kept for the saves and drains
after the listener, 6.4 s for the backend stop (the stdio reaper's kill reserve plus its
scheduling allowances), 2.25 s for runtime and logger retirement, and at least 100 ms for
the listener. The listener grace is what remains: 21.1 s at the 30 s default. A stage that
finishes early hands its time to the next one; a stage that runs out is logged and the stop
goes on.

**Action:** none at the default. If your configuration sets `server.shutdown_timeout` below
9 s, raise it to at least 9 s.
