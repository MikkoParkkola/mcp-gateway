change: An HTTP gateway refuses to start when `server.shutdown_timeout` is below 13 s, because the gateway's stop stages no longer each get a fresh timeout but share it as one total
action: If you set `server.shutdown_timeout` below 13 s, raise it to at least 13 s, or remove it to take the 30 s default; give an external kill deadline at least that total + 1 s, and read exit status 75 as a stop that overran

## HTTP stop: `server.shutdown_timeout` is now one total, with a 13 s minimum

**Startup:** no notice; refuses to start, only over HTTP (plain or mTLS) with
`server.shutdown_timeout` below 13 s, with this message, before it binds the listener:

> server.shutdown_timeout is 5s, below the 13s an HTTP stop needs for the listener grace,
> the saves, the backend stop and the logger flush; set it to at least 13s

(the first value is the one you set). A stdio gateway is unchanged: it keeps its own bound
and its own small values. The check runs when the HTTP gateway starts serving, so a
managed-account store may already have been opened by then.

What you gain: the gateway's stop stages are bounded by one number. Before, the listener,
the state saves, the in-flight drain, the task drain and the backend stop each started their
own timeout of `server.shutdown_timeout`, one after another, so a stop could take several
times what the setting said. Now the setting is the total from the stop signal, and every
stage is given its share of what is left.

The exit counts too. With the stages over, leftover blocking work (a task-store or audit-log
write) gets until 2 s before the total runs out; the log writer is shut down by the end of the
total, and a watchdog terminates the process 0.5 s after it, whatever is left. An external kill
deadline (a container's stop grace, a service manager's stop timeout) of
`server.shutdown_timeout` + 1 s therefore never cuts the gateway's own stop short; the shipped
templates use 31 s (docs/DEPLOYMENT.md, Stop grace).

**Exit status 75 means the stop overran.** The gateway ends with 75 instead of 0 in two cases:
blocking work was still going when its wait ended (two ERROR lines come first:
`shutdown reached the end of its wait with blocking work still running`, then
`the stop's runtime wait left blocking work running; exiting 75`), or the
log writer had not finished by the end of the total (stderr gets one
`mcp-gateway: the log writer: ...` line ending in `exiting 75`). When the watchdog ends the
process, it uses the status the stop had already settled on, or 75 if none was settled. What a
supervisor sees is a non-zero exit after a stop:

- systemd records `status=75`. After `systemctl stop` the unit is left failed and is not
  restarted. Under `Restart=on-failure`, which the shipped unit uses, a gateway stopped by a
  signal that did not come from systemd (a `kill`, a script) is started again and the unit
  returns to active; a clean exit 0 there would not have been restarted.
- Docker and Kubernetes show exit code 75 for the container. The shipped Compose file
  (`restart: unless-stopped`) and launchd plist (`KeepAlive`) restart on any exit, so 75
  changes nothing for them.

What to do: read a 75 as a pointer to the storage under the task store and the audit log,
starting from those ERROR lines. If your stops are healthy but slow, give
`server.shutdown_timeout` more time, and raise the kill deadline with it. If an alert or a
restart policy that fires on non-zero exits should ignore it, declare 75 a clean exit there
(systemd: `SuccessExitStatus=75`).

What changes: the total is split into fixed shares: 250 ms is kept for the saves and drains
after the listener, 10.4 s for the backend stop (the stdio reaper's 6 s kill reserve and a 4.4 s
graceful window),
2.25 s for runtime and logger retirement, and at least 100 ms for the listener. The
listener grace is what remains: 17.1 s at the 30 s default. The 250 ms
for saves and drains is all that is left after the listener once it has used its whole
grace, so a stop with a request still open gets a short flush, not a full one. A stage that
finishes early hands its time to the next one; a stage that runs out is logged and the stop
goes on.

**The 13 s minimum, as a timeline:** 13 s = listener 0.1 s + saves 0.25 s + backends 10.4 s
(EOF wait 2.9 s, then SIGTERM wait 1.5 s, then the 6 s kill reserve) + retirement 2.25 s.

**Backends at the 13 s minimum:** a stopping backend gets its stdin closed, then up to 2.9 s to
exit on its own (to save and flush), then SIGTERM and 1.5 s to react to it, then the kill. The
EOF wait is longer than before this change (about 2.3 s at the previous minimum). The SIGTERM
wait is held at 1.5 s so a backend that is itself a gateway, which only learns to hurry at the
SIGTERM, can take its own backends down before the kill. No measurement of real backends'
exit times backs the 2.9 s: it is the arithmetic of the shares above. When the listener and
the saves finish early, or with a larger `server.shutdown_timeout`, the unused time goes to the
backend stop (the same waits are about 14 s and 7 s when an idle listener hands over a 30 s
default); a backend that needs longer than 2.9 s to save after its stdin closes, and does not
handle SIGTERM, should be given a larger `server.shutdown_timeout`.

**Action:** none at the default. If your configuration sets `server.shutdown_timeout` below
13 s, raise it to at least 13 s. Keep any external kill deadline at `server.shutdown_timeout`
+ 1 s or more, and decide how your supervisor should treat exit status 75 (above).
