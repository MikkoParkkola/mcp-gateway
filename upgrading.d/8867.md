## HTTP stop: `server.shutdown_timeout` is now one total, with a 13 s minimum

**Startup:** no notice; refuses to start, only over HTTP (plain or mTLS) with
`server.shutdown_timeout` below 13 s, with this message, before it binds the listener:

> server.shutdown_timeout is 5s, below the 13s an HTTP stop needs for the listener grace,
> the saves, the backend stop and the logger flush; set it to at least 13s

(the first value is the one you set). A stdio gateway is unchanged: it keeps its own bound
and its own small values. The check runs when the HTTP gateway starts serving, so a
managed-account store may already have been opened by then.

What you gain: the gateway's stop stages are bounded by one number. Before, the listener,
the state saves, the in-flight drain, the task drain and the backend stop each started their
own timeout of `server.shutdown_timeout`, one after another, so a stop could take several
times what the setting said. Now the setting is the total from the stop signal, and every
stage is given its share of what is left.

What this does not yet cover: after the gateway's stages end, the process still waits up to
10 s for blocking-pool work before it exits, outside this total. A later change folds that
wait into the same budget; until then, do not size an external kill deadline (a container's
stop grace, a service manager's timeout) from this setting alone.

What changes: the total is split into fixed shares: 250 ms is kept for the saves and drains
after the listener, 10.4 s for the backend stop (the stdio reaper's 6 s kill reserve and a 4.4 s
graceful window),
2.25 s for runtime and logger retirement, and at least 100 ms for the listener. The
listener grace is what remains: 17.1 s at the 30 s default. The 250 ms
for saves and drains is all that is left after the listener once it has used its whole
grace, so a stop with a request still open gets a short flush, not a full one. A stage that
finishes early hands its time to the next one; a stage that runs out is logged and the stop
goes on.

**Backends at the 13 s minimum:** a stopping backend gets its stdin closed, then up to 2.9 s to
exit on its own (to save and flush), then SIGTERM and 1.5 s to react to it, then the kill. The
EOF wait is longer than before this change (about 2.3 s at the previous minimum). The SIGTERM
wait is held at 1.5 s so a backend that is itself a gateway, which only learns to hurry at the
SIGTERM, can take its own backends down before the kill. No measurement of real backends'
exit times backs the 2.9 s: it is the arithmetic of the shares above. When the listener and
the saves finish early, or with a larger `server.shutdown_timeout`, the unused time goes to the
backend stop (the same waits are about 14 s and 7 s when an idle listener hands over a 30 s
default); a backend that needs longer than 2.9 s to save after its stdin closes, and does not
handle SIGTERM, should be given a larger `server.shutdown_timeout`.

**Action:** none at the default. If your configuration sets `server.shutdown_timeout` below
13 s, raise it to at least 13 s.
