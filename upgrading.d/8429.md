change: A playbook step whose tool answers a result with `isError: true` is now a failed step that follows the playbook's `on_error` (abort stops the playbook; continue and retry record the step failed with its reason and a null result; retry does not call it again, because the tool already ran); before, it was recorded as completed and its error value passed to later steps as data. A step that meets a busy managed-account custody now ends the playbook under every `on_error`, and is no longer null-filled or followed by the next step: with the retryable -32603 error when no step, and no earlier attempt of that step, was tried before it, and with a non-retryable error otherwise
action: Review playbooks that relied on a failed tool's result reaching later steps, or that use `on_error: continue` to collect partial results: such a step now appears in `steps_failed` and `step_errors`. `on_error: retry` no longer retries a step whose tool reported an error; it still re-sends a step whose call ended in an error the gateway raised, and a keyed playbook call replays that step's stored failure instead of re-sending it (ADR-012), so send the call with an idempotency key; a busy-custody failure is retried by the client, not by the playbook

## A playbook step that failed in its tool result is a failed step

**Startup:** no notice

What you gain: a step whose tool reported failure (an MCP tool error, a
vendor 4xx or 5xx through a capability, a gateway dispatch error) used to be
recorded as completed. `on_error: abort` did not stop on it, and the next
step could read the error payload as if it were data. Now `on_error` decides,
as it already did for errors the step raised.

What changes:

- `on_error: abort`: the playbook stops at the failed step and answers
  `step '<name>' failed: the tool reported an error` (-32603). The tool's
  own text, at most 256 characters plus a cut marker, is in the error's
  `data.tool_text`, not in the message, so it never reaches a log line. The
  gateway forwards `data.tool_text` from any error only within that cap, a
  backend's error on a direct call included.
- `on_error: continue`: the step is listed in `steps_failed`, its reason
  (with the tool's text, capped the same way) in `step_errors`, its output
  is `null`, and the next step runs.
- `on_error: retry` no longer retries tool-reported errors. A step whose
  tool reported an error is handled as under `continue`, after one call,
  keyed or not: the tool ran and answered, so a second call could repeat its
  side effect (an email sent twice). A step whose call ended in an error the
  gateway raised (a connection or dispatch failure) is still re-sent up to
  `max_retries`, as before. In a keyed playbook call that re-send is served
  the step's stored failure instead (ADR-012), so the backend sees it once;
  in an unkeyed call it can reach the backend again. MIK-8532 (v4.1.0) takes
  that remaining case.
- A step that meets a busy managed-account custody ends the playbook under
  every `on_error`: no null result, no next step. If no step, and no earlier
  attempt of the same step under `retry`, was tried before it, the answer is
  the retryable -32603 "temporarily unavailable, retry" error, and the client
  may send the playbook again. Otherwise the answer is a non-retryable error,
  because what was tried may already have reached its backend.

What stays the same: an error a step raises (not a tool result) keeps its
handling. A refusal or a firewall block is not retried. A direct
`gateway_invoke` answers as before.
