change: A playbook step whose tool answers a result with `isError: true` is now a failed step that follows the playbook's `on_error` (abort stops the playbook, continue records the step failed with its reason and a null result, retry calls it again up to `max_retries`); before, it was recorded as completed and its error value passed to later steps as data. action: Review playbooks that relied on a failed tool's result reaching later steps, or that use `on_error: continue` to collect partial results: such a step now appears in `steps_failed` and `step_errors`. Before using `on_error: retry`, check that each step's tool is safe to call again after it failed, or send the playbook call with an idempotency key; a busy-custody failure is retried by the client, not by the playbook

## A playbook step that failed in its tool result is a failed step

**Startup:** no notice

What you gain: a step whose tool reported failure (an MCP tool error, a
vendor 4xx or 5xx through a capability, a gateway dispatch error) used to be
recorded as completed. `on_error: abort` did not stop on it, and the next
step could read the error payload as if it were data. Now `on_error` decides,
as it already did for errors the step raised.

What changes:

- `on_error: abort`: the playbook stops at the failed step and answers
  `step '<name>' failed: <the tool's message>` (-32603).
- `on_error: continue`: the step is listed in `steps_failed`, its reason in
  `step_errors`, its output is `null`, and the next step runs.
- `on_error: retry`: the step is called again, up to `max_retries`, and then
  handled as under `continue`. **A retried step is sent to its backend
  again.** A playbook call without an idempotency key can repeat a vendor
  side effect of a step that failed after its request was sent. With an
  idempotency key, each step's own key replays that step's stored failure
  instead of sending it again.
- A step that meets a busy managed-account custody ends the playbook under
  every `on_error`: no null result, no next step. If no step, and no earlier
  attempt of the same step under `retry`, was tried before it, the answer is
  the retryable -32603 "temporarily unavailable, retry" error, and the client
  may send the playbook again. Otherwise the answer is a non-retryable error,
  because what was tried may already have reached its backend.

What stays the same: an error a step raises (not a tool result) keeps its
handling. A refusal or a firewall block is not retried. A direct
`gateway_invoke` answers as before.
