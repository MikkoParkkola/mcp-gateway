change: The gateway stops a stdio backend by closing its stdin, waiting for it to exit, then SIGTERM, then SIGKILL, within the stop's budget, instead of killing its process group at once
action: Nothing for a backend that exits when its stdin closes. A backend that ignores EOF and SIGTERM now takes up to the stop's budget before it is killed; lower `server.shutdown_timeout` if shutdown must be faster

## Stdio backends get the MCP shutdown order

**Startup:** no notice, a log warning when a backend needs SIGTERM or the kill

What you gain: a stdio backend that saves state when its input closes (a
gateway run as another's stdio backend among them) keeps those writes on
every stop, eviction and requested restart.

What you keep: a backend judged dead or unresponsive (failed health probe,
failed start or handshake, timed-out call, revoked capability) is killed at
once, as before.

What changes:

- At shutdown a stdio gateway plans its backends' waits from `server.shutdown_timeout`
  (30 s by default): when its request drain was quick, each backend gets up to 16 s to exit
  after its stdin closes, then 8 s after SIGTERM, with 6 s held for the kill. An HTTP gateway gives its backends only their
  share of the stop total, which ends 2.25 s before it: about 14.5 s, 7.25 s and 6 s at
  the default when the stages before them finish at once, down to 2.9 s, 1.5 s and 6 s
  when the listener has used its whole grace (see "HTTP stop: `server.shutdown_timeout`
  is now one total"). Either way, a backend that leaves on EOF takes only as long as
  its own exit.
- An eviction or a requested restart works from a 10 s close budget instead: about
  2.5 s once stdin closes and 1.5 s after SIGTERM, with the same 6 s for the kill.
- The gateway logs a warning naming the backend when it has to send SIGTERM,
  and when it has to kill the group.
