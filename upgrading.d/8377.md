change: An open `tools/list_changed` stream (`GET /mcp` or `subscriptions/listen`) now judges its OAuth agent, client certificate and trusted proxy again at every notification, and the agent's current registry scopes decide what it is told. It closes when its bearer validates as a different agent (or an agent appears or disappears), when the certificate is past its `notAfter` or the clock cannot be read, or when its proxy is no longer in `trusted_proxies`. A stream already dead when it opens is refused with 401 "Invalid token". Each closure is counted in `mcp_view_liveness_dead_total{reason}` under `agent`, `cert` or `proxy`
action: Reconnect a client whose stream closed after its certificate expired. To end the open stream of a revoked certificate, restart with the new CRL or issue short-lived client certificates

## Open `list_changed` streams re-check agent, certificate and proxy

**Startup:** no notice

An open stream closes at its next decision when any of these holds:

- its bearer no longer validates, including a removed agent or an expired
  token (`bearer`);
- the Open WebUI assertion's window has passed (`adapter`);
- the Cloudflare Access assertion fails re-verification (`cf`);
- the agent its bearer validates as is not the one it attached with, or an
  agent appears or disappears (`agent`);
- now is past the client certificate's `notAfter`, or the clock cannot be
  read (`cert`);
- its trusted proxy's address has left `trusted_proxies` (`proxy`).

The label in parentheses is the `reason` on `mcp_view_liveness_dead_total`.
`bearer`, `adapter` and `cf` came with 4.0's per-caller hints; `agent`,
`cert` and `proxy` are new.

The certificate is valid through `notAfter` itself, the same boundary the TLS
handshake applies. rustls-webpki 0.103.15 (`verify_cert.rs:399`) refuses only
when `time > not_after`:

```rust
if time > not_after {
    return Err(Error::CertExpired { time, not_after });
}
```

A scope or policy change re-judges the stream; it never closes it. An agent
narrowed in the registry is told only of tools it can still see, and one
widened is told of what it gained. A registry change sends no notification of
its own, so the agent learns of it at the next unrelated change.

- What you lose: an open stream no longer outlives its client certificate's
  `notAfter`, a change of the agent its bearer validates as, or its proxy's
  removal from `trusted_proxies`. The client must reconnect with an identity
  that holds.
- What stays the same: a stream whose identity still holds is told exactly as
  before. Certificate revocation is still checked only at the TLS handshake,
  since the CRL is read at startup.
- What to do: nothing, unless a client treats a closed stream as fatal. It
  should reconnect.
