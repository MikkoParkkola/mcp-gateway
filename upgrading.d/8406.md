## A repeated plan is served only while every account its steps used is live

**Startup:** no notice

**What changes.** A keyed code-mode or playbook call, repeated with the same key as a retry or as a task call, is served its stored output only when every account one of its executed steps used is proven live in that request. Otherwise it answers the first such step's live refusal: the connect offer with its data, the account's resolution error, or a retryable `-32603` while custody is unavailable. Nothing is sent, no step runs again, no task is created, and the stored result is kept for a later repeat. A step the plan never ran (an untaken branch, a skipped step) is not judged, except in a record from before executed steps were kept, which judges every step the call declares (fail closed).

**What it costs.** The account walk resolves each distinct per-caller account at most once, and takes a resolution the route stage already made instead of resolving again: a live repeat measured the same resolutions with the walk as without it (the route stage's own per-tool checks, two for that plan). A custody recovery between the route stage and the walk can add one. An external (Unjudged) account costs a resolution per repeat, as a repeated single call has since MIK-8392.

**Recorded exceptions (lead rulings; the owner can overturn them).**

- CR-1: for a code-mode chain and for a playbook whose `on_error` is `continue` or `retry`, the repeat answers the first unproven step's own refusal, with its data, not the live run's answer. A live chain sends its earlier steps again and wraps the refusal as -32603 "Chain step N (server:tool) failed: …", dropping the offer's data; a live continue/retry playbook sends its other steps again and answers a success result built from their fresh outputs. Both exist only after the earlier steps run again, which a repeat must not do. The single form and abort playbooks are not excepted: their live answer is the refusal itself.
- CR-2: a repeated plan task whose managed account was revoked answers -32601 "Unknown tool: <step tool>", as a missing name does, because the stored-repeat check withholds the revoked tool before the account walk runs. A live call from an Open WebUI-bridged caller would answer the reconnect offer instead. The repeat fails closed: no stored output, no task, nothing sent. Surfacing the reconnect offer on a task repeat is a later UX follow-up.

**What stays.** Re-binding a backend to another account judges the stored output against the new binding (MIK-8407). Plans whose steps are themselves plans are not covered (MIK-8409).
