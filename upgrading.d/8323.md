## Playbooks keep the continuation envelope away from backends

**Startup:** no notice

**A playbook step can no longer carry the gateway's envelope to a backend.**
- What you lose: a playbook whose step argument names a step's `requestState`
  (`$ask.requestState`, in any spelling, alone or inside text) is refused when run,
  with an error naming the step and the reference. Loaded from a directory, it is
  skipped with a warning naming the file, step and reference. A step whose resolved
  arguments carry an envelope this gateway sealed, from a step result or from the
  caller's own inputs, fails with a refusal naming the step and reference; under
  `on_error: continue` or `retry` the step is null-filled with that reason, and a
  refusal is never retried.
- What stays the same: a playbook's declared output may still hand the envelope to
  its own client. Envelopes from another gateway, expired envelopes and ordinary
  strings pass through.
- What you gain: an envelope stays between the gateway and the client it was minted
  for. Before, a step could hand it to another backend, which could keep it and
  return it to a later call.
- What you do: if the gateway names one of your playbooks, remove `requestState` from
  that step's arguments. A step that needs the asking step's answer should read its
  other fields.

**The library's `ToolInvoker` gains a required `sealed_state_in`.**
- What you lose: code that implements `mcp_gateway::playbook::ToolInvoker` no longer
  compiles until it adds the method.
- What you do: implement `sealed_state_in(&self, value, budget)`. An invoker backed by a
  gateway delegates to `mcp_gateway::protocol::continuation::sealed_state_in` with that
  gateway's keyring; an invoker that seals nothing returns `Ok(false)`. Gateway
  operators do nothing.
