## A step that may have run is not sent again

**Startup:** no notice

What you gain: a chain or playbook step whose request may have reached the
backend, and whose answer never came back or came back as an error, runs
once. A payment, a message or a write is not repeated by the retry policy.
The step's error text ends with "it may have run: its request may have
reached the backend, so it was not re-sent; reconcile at the backend before
running it again". A gateway refusal keeps its own text. A chain step's
`attempts` is the number of calls made, not the number allowed.

What you keep: a step refused before its request was sent is still retried:
a connection that was never made (`TransportConnect`) or a backend that was
not ready (`BackendUnavailable`).

What changes in the gateway's own playbook runs: a backend's dispatch error
was already final. A step the gateway's own checks failed, after its send (a
missing signature receipt) or before it (an invalid tool name), was re-sent
under `on_error: retry` and now runs once. A failed step's `step_errors`
reason ends with the sentence above, except for a gateway refusal.

What changes for a library caller:

- `ChainExecutor`: `Transport`, `BackendTimeout`, `Http`, `Io` and
  `JsonRpcRetryable` (any status) are no longer re-sent.
- `PlaybookEngine` under `on_error: retry`: every error not raised before the
  send is final, a plain JSON-RPC error and a local error (`Internal`,
  `Config`, `Json`) included.
- Code that matched a step's error text exactly sees the sentence above
  appended.
- Under `on_error: abort`, a step that may have run ends the playbook with
  its own error: variant, JSON-RPC code, status and data unchanged. A variant
  that carries a message gains the sentence above; one that does not (`Io`,
  `Http`) is unchanged. Check `!error.is_pre_dispatch()` to know the step may
  have run. A gateway refusal (`Forbidden`, `GatewayRefused`) and a sealed
  account offer keep their text; for a gateway refusal, `is_pre_dispatch()`
  reads `false` as the conservative answer. A message without the sentence does not mean the step
  did not run.
