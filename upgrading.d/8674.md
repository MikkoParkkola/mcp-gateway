change: The OAuth login callback refuses a request whose `state` is missing or wrong with HTTP 400 and keeps the login waiting, instead of ending the login with that request's error. The `oauth.callback.state_mismatch` log event carries `has_state` and `state_len` in place of the received value.
action: None for a provider that returns `state` on its error redirects, as RFC 6749 requires. If a provider sends an error without `state`, that login now ends at its 300 s window or on cancel rather than at once; log filters that read the mismatch event's `received` field should read `has_state` and `state_len`.

## The OAuth callback checks `state` first

**Startup:** no notice

What you gain: a forged request to the login callback port, from another local process or from a web page your browser loads, can no longer end an interactive login or put its own text into the login error.

Who is affected: anyone who logs a backend in through the interactive OAuth flow.

What to do: nothing, in the usual case. A provider error that arrives without `state` now shows "State mismatch" in the browser and the login keeps waiting; cancel it, or let its window end, and retry.
