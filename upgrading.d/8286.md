## A certificate or token that names no one is refused

**Startup:** no notice; refuses the request, only for a nameless client certificate or an empty-`sub` token

What you gain: two callers can no longer be merged into one identity. A
certificate that names no subject used to be served as `mtls:<unknown>`, so
every such caller shared one idempotency namespace, one response cache, one
per-caller process and every grant written for that name. An identity
provider issuing an empty `sub` merged its users the same way.

What changes: a client certificate with no SAN URI and no CN gets HTTP 401
(JSON-RPC error -32000, "client certificate names no subject (no SAN URI and
no CN)") on every path, including `/health`. A request with no client
certificate is unaffected. An OIDC token with an empty `sub` gets 401
"Invalid token", including on public paths such as `/mcp`.

What to do: give each client a certificate with a CN or a SAN URI. Health
probes may connect without a client certificate. Remove grants whose subject
is `<unknown>`: they no longer match a nameless certificate, and would match
a certificate literally named `<unknown>`.
