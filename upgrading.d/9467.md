## Stdio stop: a backend's descendants die with it

**Startup:** no notice

**What changes:** when the gateway stops a stdio backend (an ordinary stop, a hurried stop
or a replaced backend), it first walks the backend's process tree by parent pid, freezes
every descendant with SIGSTOP, confirms each is stopped, walks again from the frozen set
until nothing new appears, then kills the frozen set and runs the group kill and the reap
as before. Before this, a backend that is itself a gateway kept its own backends in their
own process groups, so the outer group kill never reached them and they were left running.

**Who is affected:** anything a backend starts and leaves attached to it. That now dies with
the backend. A helper that has to outlive its backend must detach before the stop begins
(double fork, or `setsid` with its parent gone); a helper that detaches after the stop has
started is not reached. A double-forked daemon survives, as it always did. A process the
sweep may not signal (another user's) survives and is logged.

**Bounds:** the sweep takes at most 1.45 s of the 6 s kill reserve (700 ms to freeze, 750 ms
reserved for the kills), sized from a measurement on Linux at 4000 descendants (freeze
326 ms, kills 141 ms); the macOS cost is not measured and is covered by a 5x margin. At
most min(4096, the descriptor limit less 512) descendants are held and at most that many
pidfds (Linux) are open across all sweeps; a descendant past the cap is reported and
untouched. Nothing is left stopped on a path the gateway controls; if the gateway process
itself dies between the freeze and the kill, the frozen descendants stay stopped.

**Platforms:** Linux reads `/proc` and signals through a pidfd opened before the identity
check (kernel 5.3 or newer; older kernels and descriptor pressure fall back to a by-pid path
that stops a process, re-reads its identity and refuses to kill one it has not seen stopped).
macOS reads libproc and signals by pid under the same stop-verify-kill order; a process that
does not stop inside the freeze slice is logged as unconfirmed rather than killed blind.
Windows is unchanged (a job object already ends the tree).

**Logs:** a sweep that could not confirm every descendant gone logs the counts (residual,
unreadable, incomplete table reads, unconfirmed-stopped, stuck-stopped, stop mismatches,
rescued) and the pids, never a command line.

**Known limit:** on the by-pid paths (macOS, and Linux without a pidfd) a pid can be reused
in the microseconds between the sweep's identity read and its SIGSTOP, so the stop lands on
an unrelated process. The sweep resumes a stopped process that took the pid after the
member; if the resume fails it is logged as a stop mismatch and that process may stay
stopped until something resumes it.
