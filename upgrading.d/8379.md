## The direct route follows `/mcp`'s nonce rule

**Startup:** no notice

**A refused direct-route call keeps its nonce once something acted.**
- What changes: a signed `/mcp/{name}` call refused before dispatch gives its
  nonce back only if no credential was minted, refreshed or attempted, no tool
  list was filled and no continuation was consumed first, as on `/mcp`.
- What you do: nothing as an operator. A client re-sends such a refusal under a
  fresh nonce.

**A direct-route call that fails at the send keeps its nonce.**
- What changes: when the backend could not be reached or refused at the send
  (open circuit, rate limit, identity slots exhausted, connect failure, backend
  unavailable, or a login in progress), the nonce stays spent on both routes. A
  start attempt cannot prove no bytes left.
- What you do: re-send the call under a fresh nonce.
