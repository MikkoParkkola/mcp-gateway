- **An HTTP stop now ends the whole process inside its stop timeout.** After the stop's stages, the gateway waits at most 250 ms for leftover blocking work (it waited up to 10 s), stops its log writer within the timeout (it flushed for up to 2 s more), and exits; a watchdog ends the process at most half a second after the timeout whatever is still running. So the + 1 s grace in the shipped templates is never what ends it. A stop that overruns (a write stuck on a stalled disk, a log that cannot drain) exits with status 75 instead of 0, after its log is saved; a service manager that restarts on failure treats that as one. stdio and every other command are unchanged.
- **A SIGTERM sent right after start now drains the gateway.** The HTTP gateway installed its SIGTERM and Ctrl+C handlers only once its listener began serving, after the `Listening` line was logged. A SIGTERM in that window, for example from a script or service manager that stops the gateway as soon as it reports its port, killed the process outright: no drain, nothing saved, and a death by signal instead of exit code 0. The handlers are now installed before the gateway reports its port, so from then on a stop signal starts the normal shutdown. stdio mode already installed them early.
